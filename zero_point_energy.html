Stochastic electrodynamics says the same when done carefully: cavity shifts there "coincide with previous quantum calculations" — Lamb-shift-sized (ref. 13).

DERIVED This decides the literature's best-known extraction scheme. The Haisch–Moddel idea — atoms entering a Casimir cavity "spin down" and release energy — needs the field suppressed while emission is not: the dashed arm. Applied to hydrogen that premise predicts 1.5 eV per atom at 5% suppression and 1.3 keV at 90%; real boundaries shift ground states by micro-electronvolts (image-dipole bound: 1 µeV at 10 nm). Its one experiment (ref. 10) came in three orders of magnitude low, with material and gas trends opposite to prediction. The same balance is also an attractor from below (receipt P4): an orbit pushed under the ground state absorbs more than it radiates and is refilled within ~93 ps. Puthoff's framework argues against sub-ground ("hydrino-class") states, not for them.

8.3 What it revises in §7

REVISED Door 2, as written, does not survive. The zero-point floor is not a reservoir at 0 K or at any temperature. It is the n = 0 floor of every mode, present at every temperature, and it cancels from every net energy flux: net exchange goes as ħω[n(T₁) − n(T₂)] (receipt P7 — with and without the floor agree to 10⁻¹³). If the floor could receive heat, every isothermal enclosure would cool itself; a 1 kg copper cube would lose about 1 K per minute. If one insists on a temperature, the floor's is ħω/2kB — 240 K at 10 THz — and every oscillator's own zero-point motion sits at exactly that value, so nothing flows. A genuine door 2 needs a colder body. The sky's 2.7 K, reached through the 8–13 µm window, is the real one, and it is measured: nighttime generation at 50 mW/m² and rising (ref. 17). Evanescent near-field coupling cannot reach it. For the same reason §7b's rectifier "straddling an arrowed and an arrowless sector" is not available, since the floor is not a separable sector. Every thermometry rider (D5, D6/D6b, W1b, D7, F1) remains a good measurement; only the attribution of polarity-blind cooling changes, from "bath-sourced" to "a heat engine whose cold body must be named."

DERIVED The three Brillouin escapes of the optical-cavity paper (§1d) close for the same reason. Receipt P8 builds a tunnel junction with asymmetric electrodes and a sharp cavity line in its environment: at one temperature it carries zero net current, term by term (ref. 18). The zero-point part is matched to the junction's own zero-point motion; mode-density asymmetry changes rates, not the detailed-balance ratio; and speed is not a variable, because the balance holds frequency by frequency. That answers the optical-cavity paper's own crux — a static geometry cannot maintain a persistent gradient — and it agrees with Moddel & Dmitriyeva's own assessment that rectifying the zero-point field "violates thermodynamics principles" (ref. 10). Door 1 (one-shot) and door 3 (cosmological) stand as written. The thermometer rule stands, and is sharper.

8.4 What the measured core must then be — and the run that decides

OPEN If the §1 output is real, standard physics leaves three readings, and the programme gains from each:

  1. An unidentified non-equilibrium input — a residual gradient, light, or RF. That input is then the fuel, and naming it tells the engineering what to scale.
  2. Stored energy running down — battery-class.
  3. A genuine violation of the fluctuation–dissipation theorem — a static device delivering steady power at one temperature. That would be a larger discovery than "zero-point harvesting," and one OPH is on record against (§8.5).

One run separates them: the sealed-isothermal test (proposed as K1; to be frozen before running).

  • Setup. The article sits in a closed, light-tight, thermally lagged metal box. Two thermistors verify the gradient across it is below 10 mK. The box is kept dark for at least 24 h, and the readout passes through filtered feedthroughs.
  • Controls. A no-cavity twin and a reversed twin sit beside the article in the same box.
  • Accounting. Delivered energy is integrated past 10× the article's stored-energy bound.
  • Reading. Output that vanishes when sealed → reading 1 (reopen one channel at a time). Output that decays within the bound → reading 2. Output that persists beyond it → reading 3.

The validation slate's LN₂ point then adds the temperature dependence, with one change of wording: a temperature-flat output in a verified-isothermal enclosure is not "the zero-point channel as expected." It is reading 3.

DERIVED Where the lawful energy is, in this lane's own hardware:

9.1 Official and institutional assessments

  • DOCUMENTED DIA/AAWSAP DIRD D142, "Concepts for Extracting Energy from the Quantum Vacuum" (6 April 2010; DPIA 130622; author redacted). It is the only government assessment in the set, and it lands where §8 does:
    • No practicable extraction technique has been shown in a laboratory.
    • Casimir energies are too small for useful power.
    • The QED vacuum cannot be used up, so a continuously cycling machine is forbidden in principle, while one-shot release is allowed (§7, door 1).
    • Stochastic electrodynamics, the framework that would allow cycling, is judged inadequate for the question.
    • It quotes Jaynes: the zero-point field an atom sees is its own radiation-reaction field. That is §8's eq. (1) ⇔ eq. (4) pairing, in a government report.
    • It treats the dynamical Casimir effect as a real energy exchange paid for by the drive (§8.4, "the pump pays").
    • It records one measured null: switchable mirrors (70–400 nm gaps, Iannuzzi) did not noticeably change the Casimir force. That is the switch a tunable Casimir engine would need.
    • It carries the Haisch–Moddel cavity-hydrogen concept (US 7,379,286) at 2,150–21,500 W/cm³, explicitly conditional on a chain of conjectures. Its synchrotron test returned no result because of beam problems. §8.2 prices this premise and finds it three orders low.

That is the open-system rule in a 1992 proponent's words.
  • DERIVED Cole & Puthoff 1993 (Phys. Rev. E 48; DPIA 119258) is a one-shot result. Its worked example consumes a supply of capacitors, each used once. It is the source of door 1 and claims no cycle. The press release in the same file (119245) reads more into it than the paper does.

9.3 Greer's energy programme, 1998–2014

DOCUMENTED The archive documents a serious attempt. The Space Energy Access Systems plan (DPIA 118505, January 2003) set out the right protocol. It screened candidates, visited the top six and optioned one, then sent it to three independent labs. Watts in and out were to be measured under load, with inspection for hidden batteries. Phase I was budgeted at $100–500k, with a $100k "Z Prize". That is a K1-class test, written 23 years before this paper's K1. What happened:

  • Paper screening. Prof. Paul Czysz (aerospace, professor emeritus) evaluated five submissions in September 2002 (DPIA 119310) and passed none: a magnetic piston engine, a dual-rotor alternator, bootstrap flywheels, a "transduction device" he called a fake perpetual-motion machine, and an implosion turbine.
  • Hands-on checks (DPIA 119311, 119262, autumn 2002). An intern's replication of one device underperformed; another demonstration underwhelmed the technical reviewer; an aerospace company's test of a third was inconclusive. Planned tests of two more (a motor claimed at 1/16 W in, 2.1 W out, and Muller rebuilds) have no reported result.
  • Bearden declined a demonstration to Congress (DPIA 118838, April 2002). He gave five reasons: patent rights, the company board's control, the units being lab apparatus, a $29M finishing cost, and publicity as the "kiss of death". In 2003 he told SEAS not to plan on a demonstrator (118840).
  • Inventor friction. Many inventors did not respond, wanted payment or would only sell. One answered the 2003 Z-power offer but refused to travel for evaluation (118053).
  • The pivot. Greer's undated "New Earth Energy Incubator" draft (DPIA 119279) concedes that no inventor had overcome the obstacles. It proposes open-source, live-streamed labs at $100–200M instead.

NOT SUPPORTED "The programme found a working device" is not supported by these records. It is not contradicted either, because nothing after 2003 is documented. The programme's own experience fits the §8 picture: the protocol was right, and nothing that came through it delivered metered net power.
