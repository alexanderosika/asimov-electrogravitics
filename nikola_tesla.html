§0How to read this paper — the filter

  • Documented (green) — on the public record: patents, dated interviews, contemporaneous reporting, agreed by his biographers and his skeptics alike.
  • Open thread (amber) — a claim no independent source has confirmed or forensically killed. The honest default is open, judged from its best sources — neither true nor “debunked.”
  • Doctrine sentence (black) — the load-bearing reading this paper commits to.
  • Forensically-traced (dashed) — set aside only when the best sources identify the actual provenance of a claim as later fabrication or misattribution (a modern image, a named author's reconstruction), never merely because it sounds grand.

§2The documented genius — AC, the rotating field, resonance, the medium

The floor under everything is not in dispute by anyone. Tesla (born 10 July 1856 in Smiljan, then Austrian Empire; died 7 January 1943 in New York) built the electrical world we live in.

The polyphase alternating-current system; the induction motor, whose operating principle is a rotating magnetic field (his single most important invention, 1887–88); the Tesla coil (resonant air-core transformer); foundational work in radio and remote control; the high-voltage, high-frequency resonance experiments at Colorado Springs (1899) and the wireless-power station at Wardenclyffe (1901–06). These are textbook, patented, and world-changing — agreed by his most sympathetic biographers and his most skeptical ones alike.

Two threads in that record are the ones the framework actually inherits, and both are documented, not legendary.

The rotating field. Tesla's break-through was that you do not need a commutator flinging current back and forth; you set up a field that rotates in space, and matter follows it. The induction motor is nothing but a rotating field with a conductor caught in it. This is the seed of the whole “spin, don't throw” idea: the way to apply a force smoothly and directionally is a turning field, not an explosion.

The medium. Tesla never accepted “empty” space. For him the vacuum was a real, energetic medium — he kept the older word “ether” long after fashion dropped it — and his wireless ambition was literally to couple machines to that medium and move energy through it without wires. Wardenclyffe was that bet in brick and copper: transmit power by making the Earth and its medium resonate. The project failed for money, not for that idea.

Both threads are the network's premise stated in 1900s language. “Space is a real medium you couple to” is the spacetime lattice; Tesla's “ether” is the medium the warp drive pushes against, and his wireless coupling is the χν vertex-sharing channel in period dress. And the rotating field he invented for the motor is exactly the directional asymmetry the warp force needs — the “swirl” of warp §4 and the 12-port ring-cascade are a rotating field on the lattice. He built the rotating field for iron; the framework runs it on spacetime.

§3The flying machine — documented words, reconstructed mechanism

From about 1910 onward Tesla spoke, repeatedly and on the record, about a flying machine unlike any aeroplane. The statements are documented; the detailed mechanism usually quoted with them is a later reconstruction, and the two must be kept apart.

In the New York Herald (15 Oct 1911) Tesla said his flying machine would be “heavier than air, but it will not be an aeroplane. It will have no wings.” In a Collier's interview (1926) he called the wireless propulsion of a flying machine “perhaps the most valuable application of wireless energy,” a craft that “will carry no fuel” and be free of the aeroplane's limitations — with “neither wings nor propellers,” able to “move at will through the air in any direction,” and to “remain absolutely stationary in the air, even in a wind, for a great length of time.” Wingless, fuelless, propellantless, electrostatic, powered from the medium, and able to hover indefinitely: that is Tesla's own description, on the record.
The mechanism usually attached — multiple coils firing in rapid succession to build a rotating electrostatic field on the hull, whose charge is opposed by the earth and the air, lifting the craft — is a reconstruction, assembled by later interpreters (prominently William Lyne, §6), not a Tesla patent or paper. It is plausible and unverified, and it is not in his hand. What is in his hand is the rotating field itself (§2): Tesla did invent a rotating field; the leap to a rotating electrostatic propulsion field is the interpreters' step, not his. Mark the join honestly.
Even read at its conservative, documented core — wingless, fuelless, hovering, electrostatic, drawn from the medium — Tesla's flying machine is the closest period prefiguration of the actual warp mechanism the network has found. “Remain absolutely stationary in the air, charge opposed by the earth” is precisely the hover / support mode of warp §2: a static field the earth's gravity-gradient pushes against, holding the craft like a table holds a book, at near-zero energy. The rotating part is the swirl (§4). He described the Sport Model's Omicron (hover) mode in 1926, in plain English, without a saucer in sight.

§4The machines, and the books he kept

Tesla left two patents that bear directly on the flying-machine story, and both correct the legend in the same direction: he was an aeronautical and an energy-harvesting engineer, not an anti-gravity one, and he stayed inside conservation.

The flying-machine patents — a tiltrotor, not a saucer. US 1,655,113 and 1,655,114, “Apparatus for Aerial Transportation” (both granted 3 Jan 1928; among his last), describe a VTOL “helicopter-plane”: it rises vertically, then tilts to fly forward like an aeroplane, with the cabin on trunnions so the occupants stay upright through the rotation. It is widely credited as an early tilt-rotor / tilt-wing concept. It was never built, and it is emphatically not an antigravitational or electrostatic disc — its “overlapping discs” are a propulsion-and- attitude mechanism, the most likely innocent seed of the later “Tesla saucer” confusion.
Radiant energy — harvesting, not free energy. US 685,957, “Apparatus for the Utilization of Radiant Energy” (1901), charges a condenser from an insulated plate exposed to sunlight, ultraviolet, or other ionising radiation, then discharges the stored charge to do work. Read by its own claims it is a collector of naturally available radiation — a primitive cousin of a solar cell — not a perpetual-motion or over-unity device. His wireless power was transmission of energy generated elsewhere. Neither claim asks for something from nothing.
This is the cleanest difference between Tesla and Schauberger, and it is the difference the network cares about most. Schauberger reached for the self-running machine and crossed the conservation line his own benches — un-instrumented to the end — could not carry. Tesla — harvesting on one side, transmitting on the other — kept the books. He is the historical figure who has both the right medium-coupling intuition and the right arithmetic: hover can be ≈ free (you hold a static field, doing no work, warp §2), energy is paid for when you accelerate, and nothing is ever conjured. He is the bridge that does not have to be apologised for.

§5The Dynamic Theory of Gravity — announced, never delivered

On his 81st birthday (10 July 1937), Tesla issued a prepared statement saying he had “worked out a dynamic theory of gravity” that he hoped soon to give the world. The fragments he described frame gravity as an effect of the medium: a phenomenon of the ether, built from transverse and longitudinal electromagnetic waves, with the medium having “an indispensable function” in gravity, inertia, and the motion of bodies — explicitly offered as an alternative to Einstein's curved spacetime, which Tesla rejected.
He never published it. There is no surviving mathematical statement of the theory, and — beyond his own announcement — no independent evidence that the equations were ever worked out. He died in January 1943 with the theory undelivered, and the rumour of seized papers (§6) attaches to it but does not supply it. So the Dynamic Theory of Gravity is a genuine open thread: a documented announcement of a real intuition, with the content missing. We can report that Tesla aimed at gravity-from-the-medium; we cannot inherit a derivation that was never written.
The intuition is, remarkably, the network's: gravity is not a separate geometric entity but a property of the medium — in the framework, energy stored on a lattice edge lengthens it, and that length-gradient is gravity (the elastic-metric law, warp §12). Tesla's “gravity is an effect in the ether, not curvature” lands on the same side of the same argument. But this is a matching intuition, not a usable theory transferred: the framework derives its weak-field gravity (including the factor-of-two light-bending) from the scalar elastic law itself; Tesla's contribution is the correct instinct, announced, not the math, which is gone. Honesty: a shared hunch, not a borrowed result.

§6The saucer that is not his

Now the forgery, named — because the filter must cut this way too. A “Nikola Tesla flying saucer patent,” complete with a disc-shaped craft that does look strikingly like a Sport-Model saucer, circulates widely online. It is not real.

There is no authentic Tesla patent for a flying saucer. The disc “patent drawing” passed around is a modern fabrication / misattribution — an image made and re-posted until repetition reads as provenance — not a document from the patent record. The attribution of the saucer to Tesla traces to a named author, William R. Lyne (Occult Ether Physics: Tesla's Hidden Space Propulsion System and the Conspiracy to Conceal It; Pentagon Aliens), who argues Tesla invented the flying saucer in the 19th century and that mainstream physics is a cover-up — a conspiracist reconstruction, not a Tesla source. By the §0 test (a modern image, a named author's reconstruction, no contemporaneous document) the saucer attribution is forensically traced and set aside. Tesla's real flying-machine patent is the 1928 tiltrotor (§4).
This is the honest answer to the most natural question a reader brings to this paper — but the drawing looks just like the Sport Model. Two things are true at once. The specific image is forged; do not cite it. And the intuition behind your recognition is correct: Tesla genuinely did describe a wingless, fuelless craft that hovers and is driven by a rotating field drawn from the medium (§3), and that is a real prefiguration of the Sport Model's mechanism. So cite the documented words and the rotating field; never the saucer drawing. The recognition is real; the artifact is fake. Keeping those apart is the whole method.
